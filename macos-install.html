Install the WATTx wallet on your Mac

WATTx-Qt is the full-node wallet: it downloads and checks the whole WATTx chain itself, and lets you send, receive, stake and mine. It isn't signed by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to approve it once.

Download for Mac (.zip, 25 MB) Release notes & checksums

What you need

  • macOS 13 Ventura or newer. Check under Apple menu › About This Mac.
  • An Intel Mac, or an Apple Silicon Mac (M1–M4). Apple Silicon runs it through Rosetta, which macOS installs for you.
  • About 1 GB of free disk space and an internet connection.

Steps

  1. Download and unzip

    Download the file with the button above. Open your Downloads folder and double-click WATTx-0.1.7.7-macos-x86_64.zip if Safari hasn't already unzipped it. You get WATTx-Qt.

  2. Move it to Applications

    Drag WATTx-Qt into the Applications folder in the Finder sidebar.

  3. Open it the first time

    In Applications, hold Control and click WATTx-Qt (or right-click it), choose Open, then click Open again.

    You only need to do this once. After that it opens normally.

  4. macOS 15 Sequoia and newer

    Allow it in System Settings

    Newer macOS versions don't offer Open in step 3. Instead:

    1. Double-click WATTx-Qt. When macOS says it can't be opened, click Done.
    2. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security.
    3. Next to “WATTx-Qt was blocked”, click Open Anyway, enter your Mac password, then click Open Anyway once more.
  5. Apple Silicon only

    Install Rosetta if asked

    If your Mac asks to install Rosetta, click Install. It takes a minute, and WATTx-Qt opens afterwards.

  6. Let it sync

    On first start, keep the default data folder and let the wallet download the chain. The progress bar at the bottom shows how far it has got.

    To close it, use WATTx-Qt › Quit and wait for the window to disappear. Don't force-quit while it is syncing or loading, or it may have to start the sync over.

  7. Protect your wallet

    1. Settings › Encrypt Wallet: set a strong passphrase you won't forget.
    2. File › Backup Wallet: save a copy somewhere off this Mac, like a USB stick. Back up again after creating new addresses.

Where your data lives

The chain and your wallet are stored in ~/Library/Application Support/WATTx. To get there, open Finder, press Shift-Command-G and paste that path. Uninstalling the app does not delete this folder.

If something goes wrong

“WATTx-Qt can't be opened” or “Apple could not verify…”Use step 3, or step 4 on macOS 15 and newer.
“WATTx-Qt is damaged and can't be opened”Delete it, download the zip again, and unzip it with the Finder rather than another tool.
The app doesn't open at allCheck you're on macOS 13 or newer. On Apple Silicon, make sure Rosetta installed (step 5).
Sync stays at 0 connectionsCheck your internet connection and that a firewall isn't blocking WATTx-Qt. It connects to other nodes on port 1337.

For the command line

The node and RPC client without the wallet window are in wattx-0.1.7.7-macos-x86_64-daemon.tar.gz (wattxd and wattx-cli). macOS blocks them the same way on first run; allow them under Privacy & Security.
